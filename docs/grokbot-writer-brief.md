# BoardView — writer brief

This file is a **source-of-truth brief** for Grokbot Writer (or any copywriter).
It is not finished marketing copy. Use it to write product descriptions,
landing-page paragraphs, school-IT blurbs, and hardware listing text.

Do not invent features. Do not soften the locked-down desk. Prefer short,
precise sentences.

---

## Assignment

Write professional product descriptions of **BoardView**.

BoardView is a classroom accessibility product. A small camera looks at the
board. A small screen on the student’s desk shows that view. The teacher
controls the room from a computer.

The desk has **Wi-Fi**. That is required. The Wi-Fi is **not** general
internet access. It is only how the desk reaches:

1. the classroom cameras, and
2. the teacher’s dashboard / classroom controls.

The student **cannot** use the desk to browse the web, open other apps,
watch videos, send email, play games, visit an app store, or reach anything
else on the network. The device is a purpose-built kiosk. It does one job:
show the board.

---

## Who you are writing for

Primary readers, in order:

1. **Teachers** who have a student who cannot see the board (low vision, or
   seated too far away).
2. **School IT and administrators** who must approve a networked device on a
   desk. They will ask: does this tablet go on the open internet? The answer
   is no. It is not a tablet for general use.
3. **Parents and specialists** (vision teachers, 504 / IEP teams) who need a
   plain explanation of what sits on the desk.

Tone for all of them: calm, specific, institutional. Apple or Google product
language — light, restrained, confident. Never the “AI app” voice.

---

## Voice

**Do**

- Sound like a finished hardware product from a serious company.
- Use everyday classroom words: board, desk, camera, PIN, teacher.
- State the lock-down plainly. School IT wants this said out loud.
- Keep the light, glass, professional look in the *language* as well: short
  lines, no hype, no neon metaphors.

**Do not**

- Use dark-mode / neon / “powered by AI” language.
- Call it a tablet, iPad, Chromebook, laptop, or phone.
- Call it a toy, gadget, or “smart classroom ecosystem.”
- Promise military-grade encryption, a VPN, air-gapping, or “zero internet.”
  There *is* Wi-Fi. It is narrowly used.
- Invent FERPA, COPPA, SOC 2, or other compliance certifications.
- Name backend vendors (databases, hosts, payment processors) unless a
  technical appendix specifically asks for them.

**Register:** professional American English. Sentence case in headings if you
use them. No emoji. No exclamation points unless a headline absolutely needs
one (prefer none).

## The product in one paragraph

When a student cannot see the classroom board — because of low vision, or
because they sit at the back of the room — BoardView puts a live view of that
board on a small screen on their desk. Ceiling or wall cameras face each
surface that matters. The student enters a PIN, then sees the board and
nothing else. The teacher maps the room, assigns seats, and can hide the
board from the computer when it is not part of the lesson. Software is free
for schools. Only the physical hardware is paid for.

**Existing headline to keep or echo:** “The board, at their desk.”

---

## The three parts

Write every description around these three pieces. Do not add a fourth.

### 1. Cameras

Small cameras mount on the ceiling or wall. Each one faces something the
student needs to see: the front whiteboard, a second board, a poster.

A classroom can have as many cameras as the room needs. The desk shows one
view at a time. A **Next view** control (and the arrow keys) moves to the
next camera in the order the teacher set.

Cameras are classroom hardware pointed at teaching surfaces. They are **not**
security cameras, baby monitors, or consumer home cameras. Do not compare
BoardView to Ring, Blink, Wyze, or similar products.

### 2. Desk screen

A small screen on the student’s desk, about **5 inches** — payment-terminal
size, not a tablet.

It does exactly one job: show the board.

How a student uses it:

1. The screen sits on the desk, paired to that seat.
2. The student enters a **four-digit PIN**.
3. The board appears.
4. If there is more than one camera, they press **Next view**.
5. That is the entire student interface.

There is no teacher login on the desk. There is no home screen of apps.
There is no browser chrome, no search bar, no YouTube, no email, no messages,
no app store, no settings the student can wander into.

When the teacher hides the board (blackout), the desk shows only the
BoardView name and the time.

### 3. Teacher’s computer

Teachers use BoardView in a web browser on their own computer:

- Map the room (seats, screen desks, teacher’s desk).
- Add students and give each a PIN.
- Name each camera view.
- Hide the board for the whole class, or for one student, when it is not
  part of the lesson.

Schools create an account, then request a classroom trial or hardware.
Requests are reviewed before anything is sent or billed. There is no
self-serve shopping cart.

## Locked-down Wi-Fi (required talking point)

Treat this as a first-class product claim, not a footnote. School IT will
read it first.

### What is true

- The desk uses **Wi-Fi**. It must, or it cannot receive the camera view or
  follow the teacher’s controls (hide the board, change cameras, lock).
- That wireless link is **only** for BoardView classroom traffic:
  - live view from the room’s cameras
  - connection to the teacher dashboard / classroom session
- The desk is a **kiosk**: it is locked to BoardView. It is not a general-
  purpose computer.
- The student cannot open other sites or other applications from the desk.
- The desk is not a window onto the open internet for the child.

### How to say it (approved angles)

Use language like this. You may rewrite for rhythm; do not change the
meaning.

- “Wi-Fi connects the desk to the classroom cameras and the teacher’s
  dashboard — and nowhere else.”
- “The screen is a dedicated classroom device, not a tablet. It cannot
  browse the web or open other apps.”
- “Purpose-built. Locked to BoardView.”
- “No browser. No app store. No other destinations.”
- “The student sees the board. That is the only thing the device is for.”

### How not to say it

Do **not** write:

- “No Wi-Fi” / “offline” / “air-gapped” / “never touches a network.”
  False: Wi-Fi is required.
- “Unhackable,” “military-grade encryption,” “bank-level security,”
  “zero-trust mesh,” “blocks all packets.”
  Overclaim. We are describing product purpose and kiosk lockdown, not a
  security audit.
- “The student has internet on their desk, but it’s filtered.”
  Wrong model. They do not have internet. They have BoardView.
- “Kids can’t get in trouble online.”
  Too casual, and it implies the device *could* go online if someone tried
  the usual way. Frame it as: there is no general internet path in the
  student experience.

If you need a single sentence for a spec sheet:

> Wireless: Wi-Fi, used only to reach classroom cameras and the teacher
> dashboard. The desk is a locked kiosk and does not provide general
> internet, a web browser, or other applications.

## Facts you must not invent

Do not specify, unless a later brief adds them:

- Exact price, shipping times, or inventory counts
- Battery life, weight, or materials of a finished commercial enclosure
- Camera resolution, frame rate, or “4K”
- Operating system brand on the desk (do not say iOS, Android, ChromeOS,
  Windows as the student-facing platform)
- Encryption protocols, certificates, or compliance logos
- That the live camera feed is already shipping in every classroom (the
  product *is* a live view of the board; do not write a hardware teardown)

If a listing needs a connectivity line and nothing else fits, use the spec-
sheet sentence in the Wi-Fi section above.

## Suggested deliverables

Write each as a separate block, labeled, so they can be copied independently.

1. **Headline** (3–8 words). May keep “The board, at their desk.”
2. **One-sentence description** (≤ 30 words). Must mention the desk and the
   board. Should imply it is not a general tablet.
3. **Short product description** (40–70 words). For a listing or request
   page. Must include locked-down Wi-Fi.
4. **Standard description** (90–140 words). For a school or specialist.
   Cover cameras, desk, teacher controls, PIN, and that Wi-Fi does not mean
   open internet.
5. **IT / administrator paragraph** (80–120 words). Lead with: networked
   classroom kiosk; Wi-Fi only to cameras and teacher dashboard; no student
   browser or other apps.
6. **Parent / IEP-friendly paragraph** (60–100 words). Plain language.
   Dignity of the student. Not a toy, not a surveillance camera on the child.
   The cameras face the board.
7. **Three bullets** for a spec-like “What you get”: camera, desk screen,
   teacher computer — matching the product’s three parts.
8. **Words not to use** — a short list you actually avoided, so editors can
   check.

Optional extra, only if it stays accurate:

9. **Blackout, one line** — teacher can hide the board; the desk keeps the
   name and the time.

## Positioning notes

- BoardView is **accessibility hardware for class**, not edtech software with
  a feed of apps.
- The cameras watch **the board**, not the student.
- The desk is closer to a **payment terminal** or dedicated classroom
  display than to a consumer tablet.
- Dignity: the student is keeping up with the same lesson as everyone else.
  Do not write pity, “special needs gadget,” or “so they don’t fall behind”
  in a patronizing register. “Keep up in class” is acceptable if it stays
  factual.
- Contrast that *is* allowed: it looks like it might be a small tablet;
  it is not. It cannot do what a tablet does.

---

## Quality bar

A good draft:

- Could be read aloud in a school board meeting without embarrassment.
- Makes IT nod: Wi-Fi exists; general internet does not.
- Never makes BoardView sound like an AI product, a security camera system,
  or a 1:1 student laptop program.

When in doubt, cut. Accuracy over flourish.
